# NOTES 2026-07-18

## Restarted From Protocol Audit

The prior Sun2024 pipeline used artificial 40-event blocks derived from event
order. This was invalid because the public forty-target CNT annotations do not
provide usable block boundaries, and the paper's offline leave-one-out protocol
is defined by repetitions per target.

All pseudoblock runner files and all outputs were moved to
`_legacy/20260718_invalid_pseudoblock_pipeline/`. They are excluded from
current analysis and paper comparison.

## Verified Dataset Structure

- Local data include only `Data of 1Target` and `Data of 40targets offline`.
  The paper's ten-subject online training plus validation recordings are not
  present.
- Offline subjects 10-14 contain exactly five repetitions of every target and
  are the only local records directly matching the paper's 200-trial offline
  count.
- Offline subjects 1-9 contain exactly eight repetitions of every target and
  can use repetitions 1-5 for the unified five-fold paper-comparable smoke;
  repetitions 6-8 remain an explicit extended-data option.

The new adapter assigns each trigger an occurrence index within its target.
The new classifier smoke uses target-repetition LOO: each test fold contains
one trial from every target, with four training trials per target in the
paper-matched five-repetition subset.

## Corrected Smoke Result

`protocol_smoke_20260718` verified the two local offline structures and wrote
a raw time/frequency event check. `offline_target_repetition_smoke_s10_20260718`
then ran subject 10 with five target-repetition folds, all 64 EEG channels,
0 s onset shift, and the documented comb-filter assumption (`f0=50`, `Q=35`).
It produced exactly 200 predictions per method, with 40 unique targets in
every held-out repetition. The resulting 0.6 s TRCA/eTRCA accuracies were
0.0900 and 0.1300.

This is a valid protocol smoke but not an acceptable reproduction result.
No full run is authorized from it. The remaining investigation is limited to
event timing, the undocumented author comb-filter parameters, and the paper's
O/S group plus matrix-filter bTRCA/ebTRCA implementation.

## Focused Diagnostics After Corrected Smoke

An exploratory timing scan was initially run for subject 10. It is not part of
the current protocol and must not be used to select a latency. The task now
uses the fixed project-standard 0.14 s trigger-to-EEG offset; the paper does
not publish a separate latency correction.

The exploratory eTRCA errors for subject 10 were not dominated by the paired
left/right frequency swaps: only 2 of 158 errors were the corresponding swap
target. A specialized receiver cannot be validated merely by counting swap
errors from the generic eTRCA baseline.

A 2 s, no-training dual-frequency CCA diagnostic showed marked
subject variation in the paper-matched five-repetition subset:

| Subject | CCA top-1 | Median true-target rank / 40 | True target in top 5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | 0.050 | 15 | 0.180 |
| 11 | 0.395 | 2 | 0.845 |
| 12 | 0.130 | 6 | 0.490 |
| 13 | 0.325 | 2 | 0.820 |
| 14 | 0.245 | 3 | 0.665 |

This rules out a global trigger-label failure. Subject 10 is an unusually weak
record, consistent with the paper's online table where subject 10 is also the
lowest participant. In a correct target-repetition TRCA/eTRCA smoke for
subject 11, TRCA/eTRCA were 0.2300/0.2400 at 0.6 s and
0.4650/0.4450 at 1.0 s. Generic template methods are therefore a baseline,
not evidence that the paper's bTRCA/ebTRCA has been reproduced.

## Basic TRCA Tuning On Good-Subject Diagnostic Set

The paper-comparable protocol is now fixed as repetitions 1-5 for every
target, held out target-wise as five folds. This applies to subjects 1-9 as
well as subjects 10-14; the former subjects' repetitions 6-8 are not used in
this comparison. Tuning was performed first on the diagnostically responsive
subjects 11 and 13, without authorizing a full cohort run.

At 64 channels and five standard filter-bank bands, an exploratory 0.20 s onset shift
the assumed 50 Hz/Q=35 comb made only small differences from DC removal plus
filterbank. At 2.0 s, TRCA was 0.800/0.920 (comb) versus 0.765/0.920
(filterbank) for subjects 11/13. The comb parameters remain an undocumented
assumption: the paper says that forty-target data used comb filtering, but
does not publish its settings.

The first 8-90 Hz band was materially better than three or five bands in this
diagnostic set. With 64 channels, DC removal, one band, and an exploratory 0.20 s shift,
mean TRCA accuracy over subjects 11 and 13 was 0.3975, 0.6050, 0.7750, and
0.8825 at 0.6, 1.0, 1.4, and 2.0 s. The corresponding five-band values were
0.3450, 0.5600, 0.7300, and 0.8425. The nine-channel occipital subset did
not improve the 64-channel result.

That exploratory timing scan was superseded before any full-cohort work. All
current and future preprocessing, filter-bank, and classifier comparisons use
the fixed 0.14 s offset. No full-cohort report may use diagnostic subjects as
its evaluation population.

The public runner now hard-codes this 0.14 s offset rather than exposing a
latency tuning option. All non-fixed-latency result directories are segregated
from candidate results.

## Fixed-0.14 s Baseline Check

With fixed 0.14 s timing, 64 channels, five target-repetition folds, and
subjects 11/13 only, the best ordinary TRCA setting at 0.6/1.0/1.4 s was two
standard bands (0.3900/0.6150/0.7875 mean accuracy). The best ordinary eTRCA
setting was a single band without the assumed comb filter
(0.4400/0.6400/0.7900). Q=10/35/70 comb choices did not yield a stable
short-window advantage.

The article's Fig. 6 was visually checked directly. Its ordinary TRCA/eTRCA
0.6 s curves are approximately 0.4-0.45, while the substantially higher
curves belong to bTRCA/ebTRCA. Therefore the fixed-latency generic baseline
is in the paper's approximate range. This does not validate bTRCA/ebTRCA:
their group mapping and matrix-filter construction still require an
implementation-specific check before any full-cohort run.

## Comb Filter Reproduction Boundary

The paper says only that the forty-target data received comb filtering to
remove DC and intermediate-frequency interference. It provides neither an
implementation nor filter parameters. The authors' public repository
`THU-INFERON/Data-for-Efficient-Dual-Frequency-SSVEP-Brain-computer-Interface-System`
was cloned and checked on 2026-07-18: it contains only `README.md` and
`LICENSE`, and links back to the data portal. It contains no analysis code.

The prior 50 Hz `iircomb` with arbitrary Q was an Arena sensitivity assumption,
not an author reproduction. Its result directories are excluded from candidate
results, and `run_offline.py` now defaults to the standard filter-bank path.
Any future author-comb result must be labelled unverified unless the authors
provide their code or exact filter definition.

## Empirical Comb Check On Shared CNT Data

Superseded on 2026-07-18: this section incorrectly interpreted the paper's
comb filtering as a 50 Hz power-line notch. The paper's reference to DC and
intermediate-frequency interference does not establish that interpretation.
The associated script and outputs were moved to
`_legacy/20260718_powerline_notch_misread/` and must not be used for the
current comb-filter design. The replacement analysis is target-conditioned:
it evaluates the codebook's paired stimulus frequencies, harmonics, and
candidate interaction components against the recorded FFT/PSD response.

`analyze_comb_filter.py` was run on subjects 11 and 13, repetitions 1-5, using
the fixed 0.14 s timing. It compares 2 s cue-period and stimulation Welch PSD
over all 64 EEG channels. Both subjects show a deep, stable 50 Hz null rather
than a 50 Hz line peak: cue/stimulation power at 50 Hz was
-133.62/-138.38 dB for subject 11 and -149.86/-150.64 dB for subject 13. The
apparent 48.5/51.5 Hz narrow peaks are the shoulders surrounding that null.
No cross-subject, cross-state positive narrow line was identified that can be
removed without also risking task-related frequencies.

A controlled fixed-0.14 s classification sensitivity check used the current
ordinary-TRCA diagnostic setting (64 channels, two bands, subjects 11/13,
five target-repetition folds). At 0.6 s, mean TRCA was 0.3900 without comb and
0.3875/0.3850/0.3875 with assumed 50 Hz comb Q=10/35/70. At 1.0 s, it was
0.6150 without comb and 0.5900/0.6050/0.6100 with comb. These results provide
no support for a second 50 Hz comb on the shared CNT files. The current
standard filter-bank baseline is therefore the empirically preferred path;
the source data may already embody the authors' acquisition/preprocessing
notch, but that remains an inference rather than a documented claim.

## Target-Conditioned Spectrum And Passband-Comb Check

The power-line interpretation above is superseded. The correct next question
is which target-coded frequencies should be retained, not which common line
should be notched. `analyze_target_comb_spectrum.py` uses the forty-target
codebook and the fixed 0.14 s timing to compare each target's 2 s FFT/PSD
against non-pair targets. The swap pair is excluded from this contrast because
it deliberately shares the same frequency pair.

Across subjects 11/13, the components with consistently positive target versus
non-pair median contrast were the two fundamentals, their second harmonics,
and the sum frequency. Third and fourth harmonics were not consistent across
the two subjects. This motivated a passband comb retaining only
`f_left, f_right, 2*f_left, 2*f_right, f_left+f_right`.

Two fixed-0.14 s, five-fold, 0.6 s ordinary-TRCA sensitivity smokes were run:

| Comb definition | Half-width range | Best S11/S13 mean accuracy | Result |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Pair-specific passband union | 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 Hz | 0.2850 (1.5 Hz) | Below standard TRCA |
| Global union of all codebook components | 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 Hz | 0.3425 (1.0 Hz) | Below standard TRCA |
| Standard two-band TRCA | n/a | 0.3900 | Current generic baseline |

The pair-specific comb is particularly weak because the 0.6 s observation
contains only 1.67 Hz physical frequency resolution and many codebook
frequencies are much closer than that. The global union is more stable but
still does not improve ordinary TRCA. These are valid negative results, not
author-comb reproduction and not candidates for a full-cohort run. The next
algorithmic issue is bTRCA/ebTRCA's target-matrix implementation, for which the
paper does not publish executable code and whose printed scoring dimensions
are not sufficient to recreate unambiguously.
